# Review context
Structural diagnostic previously generated from the complete 41-page manuscript. Use as hypotheses to challenge, not as established findings. Every legal source remains unverified unless independently checked.

The central proposal: statutory conditional exclusive channel for derivative professional-service harms to a Professional Service Principal (PSP), with compulsory upstream evidence, cooperation, indemnity, insurance, and staged resolution. The paper's claimed novelty is the professional undertaking as the selection criterion, rather than control of AI, combined with mandatory supplier recourse.

Major challenges to test in the exact text:
1. Is exclusivity non-vacuous? Model clause says claims arise solely from a PSP duty; independent supplier duties remain intact. What actual supplier claims are excluded?
2. Why is exclusive downstream channelling needed if minimum contract/evidence obligations could be imposed without it? Compare against mandatory upstream terms plus ordinary concurrent liability.
3. Does the thesis slide among extinguishing claims, sue-first ordering, and a nonexclusive primary channel?
4. Are the five predicates workable and adjudicable, and who bears proof, timing, appeal, insolvency and limitation risk?
5. Does the evidence from only three published vendor terms support a statutory diagnosis? Are contract examples representative of enterprise procurement?
6. Do recourse rights withstand supplier insolvency, offshore enforcement, supply-chain complexity and supplier bargaining power?
7. Do fundamental access-to-court, constitutional, procedural and legislative-competence limits undermine the proposal?
8. Does the non-delegable duty analogy establish continuing PSP responsibility but NOT supplier immunity? Is the nuclear analogy too distant?
9. Are EU AI Act, PLD and GDPR article attributions, dates and exceptions actually correct and current? Preserve product and data claims.
10. For India, how do DK Gandhi, professional discipline, medical consumer fora, judiciary immunities, DPDP phased commencement, and enforceability of mandatory indemnity affect JGLR fit? India should do argumentative work, not be an appendix.
11. Does the paper's language repeatedly announce problems it then does not actually resolve? Identify exact instances.
12. Check OSCOLA-based citations, missing pinpoints, inconsistent footnote references, abandoned acronym taxonomy, figure-versus-text mismatch, British English, 250-word abstract and five keywords.

The article compares US, EU and India, with a UK case as a central analogy. It contains a figure representing downstream and upstream liability flows. Evaluate every assigned paragraph on its own merits; do not assume this map is accurate.
